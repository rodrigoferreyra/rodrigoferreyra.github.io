When I started working with Unaric, the company was growing quickly through acquisitions. Along with its products came their documentation: websites, PDFs, and content created by different teams and writers over time.

The result was a familiar problem in a growing software company: there was plenty of documentation, but it didn't necessarily feel like it belonged to the same organization. Different products had different structures, writing styles, terminology, navigation, and approaches to explaining the product. Some content was also simply legacy material that no longer reflected how the products worked.

The goal was to bring all of this together without losing the product-specific knowledge each team had built up.

Unaric selected Archbee as the documentation platform, with each product getting its own documentation space. I led the migration across roughly a dozen product documentation sets, working with product teams and subject-matter experts along the way.

The work quickly became more than moving content from one place to another.

I developed a documentation style guide covering voice and tone, terminology, formatting, headings, article structure, and information architecture. This gave me a consistent framework for making decisions across products while adapting the documentation to Unaric's identity.

I reviewed the existing content article by article, deciding what was still useful, what needed to be rewritten, and what could be removed. I tested instructions myself where possible, which often revealed that technically correct information could still be explained more clearly or structured differently.

The result was documentation that was easier to navigate and maintain—and, importantly, looked and felt like Unaric documentation, rather than a collection of unrelated product manuals.

Once the migration was underway, it became clear that a unified documentation site was only part of the solution. If new documentation was going to remain consistent, documentation needed to become part of how products were developed.

Together with the teams, we established a workflow connecting product development and documentation. Product work could flag documentation requirements directly in Jira, while Slack was used to clarify specific requirements. I worked with the relevant team members to understand changes, write or update the documentation, get it reviewed by subject-matter experts, and publish it.

Documentation became something considered before a feature shipped—not something added afterward. I also gained the authority to push back when the information needed to document a feature wasn't available yet.

Over more than two years, what began as a documentation migration evolved into an ongoing role supporting Unaric's product teams.

Around a dozen product documentation sets have been migrated or created, with legacy content removed, existing material reorganized and rewritten, and new documentation produced as products evolve.

More importantly, the documentation became a coherent part of the Unaric product experience. What started as a collection of documentation inherited from different products became a system that could grow with them.
